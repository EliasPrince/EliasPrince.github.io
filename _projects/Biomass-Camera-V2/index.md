## Why it exists

Fish farms run on two numbers that are surprisingly hard to measure: how many fish are in the pen, and how much feed they need. Overfeeding and inconsistent sales make a farm difficult to scale. The camera gives operators a daily biomass number without anyone handling a fish. To be worth anything, it also has to survive life at sea and fit into the farm's routine rather than add to it.

{% include figure.html src="BMCV2_Annotated_Fish.webp" caption="A detection frame from the onboard model, with fish outlined and measured in real time." %}

## What I did

I lead the mechanical design of the camera, from concept through launch. At its core is a sealed pressure housing with high-power compute inside, so it has to shed heat while living in seawater, taking fish strikes, and being handled on pen decks and skiffs. My work spans the design and analysis across structural, thermal, optical, sealing, and exterior components, as well as the build and acceptance process that makes production units repeatable. The program had no systems engineer, so I also wrote the requirements and traced them through risk and verification, and I run the mechanical test program.

## Outcome

A newly formed cross-functional team took the product from definition to commercial launch in just over a year. I field-tested two prototypes at commercial farms alongside the current camera, and the full chain worked end to end: into the pen, video captured, detection on board, results in the cloud, with no leaks and no mechanical failures.
